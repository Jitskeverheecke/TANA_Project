\chapter{Dynamical analysis of a dark-matter halo in cosmological and hydrodynamical simulations}
Simulation forms an important tool in theoretical astrophysics, allowing to model the complex structure of processes of the universe. Moreover, with the increase in computational power, simulations have become more detailed and accessible for application in astrophysics.

The IllustrisTNG project steers towards a predictive theory of galaxy formation, and aims to model the universe from the very beginning after the Big Bang to the present day.
In this project a short and quantitative analysis of a cosmological simulation that was performed within the framework of IllustrisTNG is presented. Here, the focus is laid on the analysis and discussion of the properties of a single subhalo, thus a cutout with ID 344137. 

This dataset contains $N = 268525$ dark matter particles with in a cubic volume of side length $L = 35000 h^{-1}$kpc in physical length at redshift of $z\approx 0$. Here, the quantity $h=0.6774$ refers to the dimensionless Hubble parameter. Each individual particle the ensemble has a mass of $m_\text{DM} \approx 10^{6.73} M_\odot$ with $ M_\odot$ the solar mass, 3D position and corresponding velocity attributes. The system was simulated in a $\Lambda$CDM setting with $\Omega_m = 0.3089$ and $\Omega_\Lambda = 0.6911$. Even though baryonic processes will not be discussed in this work, it is worth mentioning that the full simulation also included gas cells (baryonic matter) as well with a baryonic content of $\Omega_b = 0.0486$. Other cosmological parameters and quantities of the simulation that are relevant for this work is summarized in \cref{tab:cosmological_parameters}.

This symmetry behaviour can be quantified by decomposition of the distribution of the dataset on the spherical basis as is discussed as follows. 
The number density profile ($n(\textbf{r})$) of a distribution of point particles with coordinates $\textbf{r} = (x,y,z)$ is generally given by 
\begin{equation*}
    n(\textbf{r}) = n(r,\theta,\phi)=\frac{1}{V}\sum_{i} \delta(\textbf{r} - \textbf{r}_i)
\end{equation*}
where the sum runs over all particles $i$ at position $\textbf{r}_i$ within a volume $V$. In the framework, the dataset is binned in $N=28$ spherical shells of thickness $\Delta r$ and radius $r$ around the center of mass of the system, containing particles i with spherical coordinates $(r_i, \theta_i, \phi_i)$. For each shell, the spherical harmonics decomposition of the distribution field is performed by the virtue of \cref{eq:spherical_harmonics_expansion}
\begin{equation*}
    n(\textbf{r}) = n(r, \theta, \phi) = \sum_{l=0}^{\infty} \sum_{m=-l}^{l} n_{lm}(r) Y_{lm}(\theta, \phi)
    \label{eq:spherical_harmonics_expansion}
\end{equation*}
with $Y_{lm}(\theta, \phi)$ the spherical harmonics basis functions of order $l$ and degree $m$, and invoking the orthonormality. The coefficients $n_{lm}(r)$ are then further used to compute the power spectrum $P_l$ of the distribution as a function of the multipole order $l$ via
\begin{equation}
    P_l = \frac{1}{2l+1} \sum_{m=-l}^{l} |n_{lm}|^2.
\end{equation}
For a perfectly spherically symmetric system, only the monopole term ($n_{00}$) with ($l=0, m=0$) in the expansion survives. However, as shown in \cref{fig:sphericity_analysis_density}, the power originating from the dipole ($l=1$) and quadrupole ($l=2$) as well as $l=3$ survives. Eventhough these power survives, it can be seen that this remains in a good approximation constant for distances below approximately $100$ kpc and relatively smaller than $P_0$. The increase in the power for radii larger than $100$ kpc can be attributed to outliers and uncertainities in the density field due to less data points.

Thus, in a good approximation, the dark matter distribution in the subhalo can be considered spherically symmetric, which allows for simplications in the further analysis of the system.

Carrying on with a spherical distribution in mind, the mass density profile ($\rho(r)$), hence forth referred simply to as density, is directly related to the spherical number density via $\rho(r) = m_\text{DM} n(r)$ with $m_\text{DM}$ the mass of an individual dark matter particle. The density $\rho(r)$ or equivalently $n(r)$ of the discrete dataset is obtained by binning the particles in radial distance of radius $r$ and thickness $\Delta r$ in the center of mass of the system and counting the number of particles in each bin. This is simply achieved by yse of the \textit{numpy} \texttt{histogram} function with 28 bins logarithmically spaced between the minimum and maximum radial distance of the particles. Dividing the number of particles in each bin by the corresponding shell volume and multiplying with the particle mass yields the density profile $\rho(r)$. Mathematically, this process amount to 
\begin{equation}
    \rho(r_i) = \frac{m_\text{DM}}{V_i} \sum_{r_j \in \text{shell } i} 1 = \frac{3 \cdot m_\text{DM}}{4\pi (r_{i+1}^3-r_{i-1}^3) } \sum_{r_j \in \text{shell } i} 1.
\end{equation}

The resulting density profile is shown in \cref{fig:sphericity_analysis_density} (\textbf{left}) on a log-log representation.
The density profile shows a typical behaviour of a dark matter halo, with a steep decrease in density for larger radii ($r\geq 100$ kpc) and a cusp, i.e flattening for radii approaching zero.
